Resolving Business Disputes: When to Litigate and When to Settle

Commercial disagreements rarely arrive at a convenient moment. For founders preparing for investment, a dispute can distract leadership, slow diligence and introduce uncertainty into conversations that already require clarity and trust.

The question is not whether conflict should be avoided — it is whether the path you choose protects the business, the cap table and the relationships that matter for the next stage of growth.

When settlement is often the better default

Settlement can preserve operating momentum, limit disclosure risk and reduce the management time absorbed by formal proceedings. Where the underlying issue is contractual, operational or relational, a structured negotiation may resolve the matter faster than court timelines allow.

  • The commercial relationship still has strategic value
  • Exposure is containable and documentation is relatively clear
  • Leadership bandwidth is needed elsewhere — including fundraising

When litigation may be necessary

Litigation becomes more defensible when precedent, enforcement or fundamental rights are at stake, when the counterparty is unlikely to engage in good faith, or when delay itself creates irreversible harm to the company or its stakeholders.

Even then, the decision should be made with eyes open: cost, distraction, reputational perception and the impact on investor diligence all belong in the same conversation.
